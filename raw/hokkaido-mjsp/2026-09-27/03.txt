Drawing from her filmmaking practice in Japan, Singapore, and Germany, she shared fascinating insights into issues of labor, belonging, identity and precarity. Dr. Wadhwa discussed questions of reflexivity, positionality and consent as a researcher in visual ethnography. The analysis of diverse cases from multiple contexts was followed by animated questions from students and faculty. Dr. Wadhwa's workshop on 31 July introduced basic techniques for documenting migrant experiences using accessible tools such as phone cameras, advice for conducting of interviews and discussed the role of visual documentation in representing lived experiences of migrants in different contexts. Thank you, Dr. Wadhwa, for your thought-provoking lecture! #hokkaido #sapporo #hokkaidouni #hokkaidouniversity #MJSP #MJSPLectureSeries 07, 13, 2026 As part of the MJSP Spring/Summer Lecture Series 2026, on July 10 Prof. Carolin Funck (Hiroshima University) delivered a lecture entitled "Island Tourism in Japan: Beyond Sun, Sand and Sea". Introducing her multiple research on islands across Japan, she provided fascinating insights into the demographic challenges, new forms of tourism development such as high value-added tourism and differences in travel between Japan and other countries. The analysis of diverse case studies was followed by animated questions from students and faculty. Thank you, Dr. Funck, for your thought-provoking lecture! #hokkaido #sapporo #hokkaidouni #hokkaidouniversity #MJSP #MJSPLectureSeries 07, 01, 2026 As part of the MJSP Spring/Summer Lecture Series 2026, on June 25 Prof. Paul Hansen (Akita University) delivered a lecture entitled "A Cosmopolitical Countryside: One Health, Wellbeing and Ikigai on the Frontiers of Japan". Focusing on Tokachi in East Hokkaido, he provided fascinating insights into regional history, ethnographic descriptions of humans and non-humans and introduced numerous concepts from social theory such as heterotopia and meshworks. The analysis of diverse case studies was followed by animated questions from students and faculty. On June 26, in a workshop entitled "*So, You Want to Do Fieldwork Eh? *: A Workshop on Embodied Knowing and Personal Limitations" Dr. Hansen addressed issues such as perception of self and others, subjectivity and objectivity drawing on multiple seminal researchers in anthropology, self-perception of skills as well as sensory aspects in fieldwork.
