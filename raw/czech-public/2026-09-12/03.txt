Your monthly budget can be higher if you rent a private flat, study in Prague or Brno, eat out often or travel regularly. Home page Get Ready for Czechia Living Costs How much will you spend? Living in Czechia can still be affordable for students, especially if you plan your budget and choose accommodation carefully. Czechia uses the Czech crown (CZK) , not the euro. The prices below are only indicative. They can change over time and may differ by city, provider and season. Food and drinks Typical prices can look like this: meal in a student canteen: 4 EUR restaurant meal: 7-10 EUR coffee in a café: 2,50 EUR bread: 1 EUR bottled water: 0,80 EUR (tap water is drinkable in Czechia) milk, 1 litre: 0,90 EUR apples, 1 kg: 1,80 EUR chicken meat, 1 kg: 6 EUR You can expect to spend around 150-230 EUR per month on food . Cooking at home and eating at university canteens will help you keep costs lower, while restaurants, cafés and food delivery can quickly increase your budget. Accommodation Accommodation will probably be the biggest part of your monthly budget. Prices vary a lot by city. If you want to keep costs lower, a student dorm or a shared flat is usually the best place to start. The lower end is more typical in smaller university cities, while the upper end is more likely in Prague or Brno. student dormitory (shared room): 160 - 330 EUR student dormitory (single room): 250 - 450 EUR room in a shared flat: 250 - 500 EUR small flat outside the city centre: 500-900 EUR small flat in the city centre: 600-1000 EUR Explore Accomodation Options in Czechia Find Your Home Public transport and travelling Public transport in Czechia is reliable and student-friendly . In most university cities, students can get discounted long-term passes . A yearly student public transport pass can cost roughly 50-100 EUR, depending on the city and tariff rules. Always check the current price with the local transport provider. You may also use trains and buses to travel between cities or to nearby countries. Some examples of prices on one-way tickets based on prices in May 2026: train Prague-Brno: 16 EUR train Prague-Berlin: 37 EUR train Brno-Vienna: 18 EUR bus Prague-Krkonoše mountains: 4 EUR bus Brno-Bratislava: 10 EUR Leisure time and everyday costs Your spending outside classes will depend on your lifestyle Set a monthly limit for leisure activities. It will help you enjoy student life without losing control of your budget. Here are some examples based on prices in May 2026: cinema ticket: 7 EUR swimming pool admission 5 EUR gym monthly fee: 35 EUR mobile phone student tariff with unlimited data: 25 EUR Study costs Do not forget smaller study-related expenses . Use Your Student Card Use Your Student Card Your student card or ISIC can help you get discounts on transport, culture, sport, shopping and selected services. Cook More and Use the Canteen Cook More and Use the Canteen Many restaurants serve daily lunch menus at lower prices. Eating in a student canteen or cooking at home is cheaper than eating out regularly. Supermarket loyalty cards and special offers can help you save on groceries. Share Your Accommodation Share Your Accommodation Shared accommodation can reduce your monthly costs. Rent, utilities and household equipment are divided between more people. Buy Second-Hand Buy Second-Hand You can often find room equipment, dishes, books and clothes second-hand in a great shape. Try student groups, second-hand shops, swap events or local marketplaces. It is cheaper and a more sustainable choice. Make the Most of Free Activities Make the Most of Free Activities Many Czech cities offer free or affordable events, museum nights, student activities, walking tours or outdoor events. Czechia is also great for hiking. You can explore marked trails across the country with very low costs. Track Your Spending Track Your Spending Set a monthly limit and check your spending regularly. A simple note, spreadsheet or budgeting app can help you see where your money goes. Smaller university towns are more affordable , especially when it comes to rent and some everyday services. As a general guide, typical living costs can vary by around 10-20% between cities. The difference in accommodation costs may be even greater.
