With a blend of ancient heritage and modern advancements, Uzbekistan provides a safe, affordable, and welcoming atmosphere for students from around the world. Why Study in Uzbekistan? Affordable Living Costs, Living expenses, including accommodation, food, and transportation, are budget-friendly. Rich Cultural Heritage, From the ancient Silk Road cities of Samarkand, Bukhara, and Khiva to the vibrant capital of Tashkent, students can explore centuries-old history, architecture, and traditions. Safe and Friendly Environment, Uzbekistan is known for its hospitality and warm-hearted people, making it an ideal place for international students to feel at home. Capital / Study city Tashkent (3.5 mln) Official language Uzbek (Russian widely spoken) Population 39 mln Demographics Uzbek 90%, Tajik 3.3%, Karakalpak 2.2%, Kazakh 1.8%, Russian 1.6% Main cities Samarkand, Namangan, Andijan, Bukhara Religion Secular state, predominantly Muslim Currency Uzbekistani Som (UZS) Time zone UZT (UTC+5) Climate Continental, hot summers with 300 sunny days, mild winters Visa for study A-1 Student entry visa required (see Visa and Arrival page) Why Study at NUU? Quality Education in English, the 1st and only QAA-accredited university in Uzbekistan. International Faculty, 60% foreign faculty from 20 countries. Presidential University, a talented pool of students. Double Diplomas, with top 200 universities Khalifa University and SWUFE. Tashkent, A Thriving Educational Hub Tashkent, the capital city, is a dynamic metropolis that serves as the center of education, business, and culture in Uzbekistan. It is home to some of the country's top universities, research institutions, and multinational companies, providing students with ample academic and career opportunities. The city also offers a rich blend of modern infrastructure, historical sites, and a vibrant social life with cafes, museums, and cultural events. You can get more information about Tashkent city through the Tashkent Guide . Cost of Living in Tashkent Monthly expense Estimated cost On-campus accommodation $125 Food (canteen, local cafes, markets) $175 Public transport (metro/bus) $18 Personal expenses $75 Estimated monthly total $315 - $470 Official Public Holidays in Uzbekistan Uzbekistan observes nine official public holidays each year. Classes, offices, and most services are closed on these dates, so it's worth planning travel and coursework around them. Holiday Date New Year's Day January 1 International Women's Day March 8 Navruz (Nowruz, spring equinox) March 21 Ramazon Hayit (Eid al-Fitr) Date varies (Islamic lunar calendar) Memorial Day (Day of Remembrance and Honor) May 9 Qurbon Hayit (Eid al-Adha) Date varies (Islamic lunar calendar) Independence Day September 1 Teachers and Mentors Day October 1 Constitution Day December 8 Ramazon Hayit and Qurbon Hayit follow the Islamic lunar calendar, so their dates shift by about 10-11 days each year. The government also periodically approves extra bridge days around major holidays, check the official calendar closer to the date.
